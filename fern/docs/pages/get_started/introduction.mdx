Schematic is a billing engine with a deep Stripe integration. It handles the billing work that general-purpose billing tools struggle with: metered AI and token consumption, credit balances, real-time usage enforcement, and the negotiated, sales-led deals that break self-serve tools.

Usage-based pricing is where most teams arrive, and seat-based, hybrid, and flat-rate plans are supported the same way. You define plans, prices, limits, and credit bundles in one catalog, and Schematic meters consumption, enforces entitlements in real time, and bills for what customers actually use.

## Why teams use Schematic

Usage-based pricing tends to stall in procurement because buyers do not trust it. They fear runaway bills, and they fear being throttled without warning. Schematic gives your customers visibility into what they have consumed, forecasts of where they will land, self-service controls, and configurable spending limits, so both sides can sign knowing usage will stay inside budget. That takes friction out of procurement and shortens the sales cycle.

**For GTM teams:** Launch plans, change prices and limits, manage trials, and grant customer exceptions, all without engineering tickets. If you can imagine a pricing change, you can ship it yourself.

**For Engineering teams:** Stop building billing infrastructure. No usage metering system to run, no billing state to reconcile across systems, no feature flag sprawl for plan logic, no manual database overrides when Sales needs an exception.

## How Schematic works with Stripe

**Schematic runs the billing engine. Stripe runs the payment rails.**

Your catalog lives in Schematic: plans, add ons, prices, usage-based billing models, credit bundles, and per-company overrides. Schematic meters consumption, enforces entitlements as customers use your product, and drives the subscription lifecycle, then keeps Stripe in step through a native two-way sync so invoicing and payments settle where your finance team already works.

What this means in practice:

- **Pricing and packaging live in Schematic** - Plans, limits, usage-based billing models, credit bundles, and customer overrides
- **Stripe processes payments** - Invoices, payment methods, retries, receipts, and tax
- **Two-way sync** - Changes on either side land on the other, with no webhook handlers to maintain and no billing state to reconcile
- **Clean separation** - GTM can change packaging and pricing in Schematic without touching Stripe configuration

This lets you run sophisticated pricing models (usage-based, credit-based, hybrid, seat-based, feature-gated) without building metering, enforcement, and reconciliation yourself, and without coupling pricing logic into your application code.

## Core concepts

### Plans and Add Ons (Catalog)

[Define your pricing structure](/catalog/overview) with plans and add ons. Each includes entitlements (features, limits, usage allowances) and pricing. Plans map to Stripe subscriptions automatically.

### Company Profiles

A centralized record aggregating company information, subscription details, and usage data. Profiles are key-based (preventing duplicates) and can reference multiple identifiers across systems.

### Feature Flags

[Control access to features](/feature-management/overview) based on company context, subscription status, or custom attributes. Flags reference entitlements from Plans to enforce access rules automatically.

### Subscriptions

Subscriptions link to Company Profiles automatically and stay in sync with [Stripe](/integrations/stripe). Companies can have membership in multiple Plans or add ons simultaneously, with the most permissive entitlements applying.

### Metering

[Track usage events](/feature-management/feature-analytics) for monetization or enforcement. Events can be attributed to companies, users, or features, supporting flexible usage-based models or packaged limits.
