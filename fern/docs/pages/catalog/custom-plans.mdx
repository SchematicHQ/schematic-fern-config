A custom plan is a plan scoped to a single company. It is built for sales-led, invoice-based deals, where a customer negotiates their own pricing and packaging and you bill them by invoice rather than through self-service checkout.

Unlike a standard [plan](/catalog/plans), a custom plan is only ever assigned to one company. It never appears in your [Components](/components/overview) checkout flow, and no other company is moved onto it. The company stays on its custom plan until you move it to another plan, or until the term ends on a plan you set not to [renew](#renewal).

![Custom plans list](../../assets/images/catalog/catalog-custom-plans-list.png)

### When to use a custom plan

Reach for a custom plan when a single company needs terms that differ from your standard catalog and you are closing the deal through sales rather than self-service. Common cases include negotiated enterprise pricing, a bespoke bundle of entitlements, or a one-off contract billed by invoice.

For other situations:

- To change a plan for everyone, publish a [new plan version](/catalog/plans#creating-a-new-plan-version) instead.
- To grant a single entitlement exception on top of a company's existing plan, use a [company override](/feature-management/overrides).
- To let a customer try the tier above theirs before they commit, use a [time-limited override](/feature-management/overrides#time-limited-overrides), which expires on its own if they don't upgrade.

## Creating a custom plan

You create a custom plan from the company it is for. On the company's profile page, open the 3-dot menu near the company name and choose **Create custom plan**.

![Create custom plan from the company menu](../../assets/images/catalog/catalog-custom-plan-create.png)

This creates a draft custom plan assigned only to that company. From there you set up its entitlements and pricing. You can start from scratch or use **Duplicate from plan** to copy the entitlements of an existing standard plan and then adjust them for this deal.

![Custom plan entitlements](../../assets/images/catalog/catalog-custom-plan-entitlements.png)

For a step-by-step walkthrough, see [Creating a Custom Plan](/catalog/guides/custom-plans). If you would rather generate custom plans from your CRM or an internal tool, the same flow is available over the API. See the [Custom Plans API](/developer_resources/custom-plans-api).

## Invoice-based activation

Custom plans are designed for an invoiced sales motion. While the plan is a draft, you review its entitlements and then **finalize** it to start billing. Finalizing generates a custom plan invoice for the company.

When you finalize, you choose an activation strategy that controls when the company gets access:

- **On payment** activates the company on the custom plan once the invoice is paid, within the configured due window (for example, 30 days). Until then, the plan stays pending.
- **Immediately** gives the company access to the plan right away, before paying. If they do not pay within the payment terms, access is removed.

![Custom plan invoice paid and active](../../assets/images/catalog/catalog-custom-plan-billing.png)

### Getting the invoice to the customer

The invoice itself is issued through Stripe, which is what gives the customer a way to pay it without you building one. Two settings control how it reaches them:

- **Send invoice** decides whether Stripe emails the invoice to the customer when it is finalized. Leave it off when your team would rather deliver it through the deal thread or a procurement portal.
- **Days until due** sets the payment window, and it is the same window the **On payment** activation strategy waits on.

Either way the invoice has a Stripe-hosted page where the customer can pay by card or by whatever methods you have enabled in Stripe, so you can pass that link along yourself rather than relying on the email.

Schematic tracks the result. The plan's **Billing** tab shows the invoice status, and when it is paid the company activates on the plan without anyone assigning it. A failed or expired invoice can be retried against the same plan, so a payment problem does not mean rebuilding the deal.

The **Custom plan invoice** card on the company's profile shows the same status. When billing setup stops before Stripe raises an invoice, the card reads "Billing setup didn't finish, so no invoice was created" under a red **Not set up** pill and lists the problems that blocked it. **Retry billing** opens the plan's **Billing** tab, where you fix the cause and try again.

<Info>In sandbox environments, Stripe does not send invoice emails by default. See the [Stripe FAQ](/integrations/stripe-troubleshooting) if an expected invoice email does not arrive.</Info>

## Renewal

The **Renewal** setting, under the plan's price when you create or edit a custom plan, decides what happens to the company when the term ends.

| Option | What happens when the term ends |
| --- | --- |
| Auto-renew | The default. The company stays on the custom plan, the period restarts, and the renewal invoice is sent on the payment terms set at finalization. |
| Don't renew · Cancel subscription | The subscription is canceled and the company moves to your [fallback plan](/catalog/lifecycle#fallback-plan). With no fallback plan set, the company has no plan and loses the custom plan's entitlements. |
| Don't renew · Migrate to another plan | The company moves to a standard plan you choose and is billed on that plan from then on. |

Neither **Don't renew** option sends a renewal invoice for the custom plan. Each needs an **End Date**, the last day the company has the plan, which you set when you finalize. The finalize step then summarizes the term, the invoices left in it, and where the company lands. A plan that doesn't renew starts its subscription with that end date, so it can't be finalized for a company that already has a subscription. Cancel the subscription first, or set the plan to auto-renew.

Renewal belongs to the plan version. To change it on a live custom plan, edit the plan and publish the new version, which also lets you switch a plan back to **Auto-renew** any time before its end date.

### Moving to another plan at term end

When you choose **Don't renew · Migrate to another plan**, pick the destination in **Move to Plan** and its period in **Billing Period**. The list offers published standard plans with a price in the custom plan's currency, and **Billing Period** offers the periods the chosen plan has a price for. **Currency** stays fixed to the custom plan's currency, because Stripe can't change a subscription's currency partway through.

On the end date, the company moves to the latest published version of the destination plan at the chosen period, without proration. Its payment terms, payment method, and per-seat quantities carry over. Add-ons, custom usage prices, and discounts stay with the custom plan, and the destination plan starts without a trial. Until then, the plan card on the company's profile names the move, such as "Ends Dec 22 · moves to Enterprise (Annual)".

While a custom plan version moves companies to a plan, Schematic refuses to delete that plan or to publish a version of it without a price at the chosen period.

## Versions and migrations

Like standard plans, custom plans are versioned. Editing a finalized custom plan creates a new draft version that you can review and publish, and you can track changes on the plan's **Migrations** tab. Because a custom plan only ever applies to one company, publishing a new version updates that single company. See [Plan Versions](/catalog/plans#plan-versions) for how versioning works.
