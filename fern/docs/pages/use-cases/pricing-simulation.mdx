<Warning>
Pricing simulation is not available yet. This page describes the expected shape of the feature and documents what is possible today. Nothing described under [Expected behavior](#expected-behavior) is currently shipping, and the details are subject to change. See the [roadmap](https://roadmap.schematichq.com/) for status.
</Warning>

A pricing change alters revenue per account, and the accounts it affects most are rarely described by the aggregate. Modeling a change against an average usage figure will not identify which specific accounts become materially more or less expensive under it.

Pricing simulation is intended to address that by evaluating a draft price against usage that has already occurred, reported per customer, before the change is published.

## Expected behavior

The intended shape is a draft plan that can be edited without publishing, evaluated against historical usage.

**Editable assumptions.** The variables driving the outcome are held separately from the plan: base fee, included credit grant per period, and cost per unit of consumption. Changing one re-runs the projection.

**Per-customer output.** One row per company reporting credits consumed, top-ups purchased, revenue generated, and margin after costs, rather than a single blended figure.

**Evaluation against real usage.** Historical consumption from the [credit ledger](/use-cases/credit-ledger) and the events behind it, rather than a synthetic distribution.

The questions this is intended to answer are whether a change grows revenue or redistributes it, how many accounts become materially more expensive, and which named accounts are most affected.

## Available today

The following are available now and cover parts of the same workflow.

**Per-company consumption data.** [Reporting usage across companies](/playbooks/usage-reporting) returns one row per company per feature for a date range, and the credit ledger covers the credit side. This is the input a manual model requires.

**Cost basis.** The cost basis on a credit type records what a credit costs you. It does not affect customer pricing, and exists so that margin analysis has a cost figure attached to the credit. See [Create a Credit Type](/billing/credit-burndown#1-create-a-credit-type).

**Plan versioning.** [Plan versioning](/use-cases/plan-versioning) keeps existing customers on the price they signed while new customers receive the new one, so a repricing does not require migrating the existing base.

**Runtime margin enforcement.** [Margin protection](/use-cases/margin-protection) enforces the limits a plan was priced around as usage occurs, rather than after the period closes.

Building the projection itself is currently manual: export the usage, apply the draft pricing externally.
